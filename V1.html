How the cruise model works

Points issued = sum of segment spending × segment earn-rate multiplier × overall earn rate.

Expected redeemable points = segment points × segment redemption-probability multiplier (capped at 100%).

Deferred revenue added = expected redeemable points × face value per point.

Revenue recognized = points redeemed × face value, assuming the associated benefit is delivered that month.

Ending liability = opening liability + new deferred revenue − recognized revenue.

Cruise-specific modeling lens

Guest spend: illustrative eligible spend on cruise bookings and onboard purchases, not total company revenue.

Sailing seasonality: toggle a synthetic peak-season demand pattern to test the impact of stronger summer and holiday periods.

Rewards fulfillment: a redemption becomes recognized revenue only when the promised cruise-related benefit is delivered.

Accounting vs. economics

Deferred revenue

Represents a remaining performance obligation, not automatically a cash expense or program loss.

Fulfillment costs

Economic costs of delivering benefits; do not subtract the face value of points again if already accounting for those costs.

Incremental contribution

Revenue caused by the program × contribution margin, minus incremental benefit, administration and campaign costs.

Important accounting and modeling caveats: This is an independent, Carnival-inspired educational simulator, not affiliated with or endorsed by Carnival Corporation or Carnival Cruise Line. All figures, including sample actuals, seasonality factors, point rules, and scenarios are invented for demonstration and do not represent Carnival Rewards program terms, Carnival financial results, or a statement of ASC 606 compliance. It assumes points are a distinct material-right obligation, that the benefit is delivered in the redemption month, and uses expected redemption value as a shortcut for initial allocation. Actual revenue allocation depends on relative standalone selling prices and contractual terms; breakage recognition, expiry, and changes in estimates may require separate accounting. Seasonality multipliers and segment behavior are illustrative rather than empirically estimated. Seasonal eligible spend should not be interpreted as GAAP recognized revenue, and segment spending shares are not customer-count shares. Monthly redemption is modeled as a fraction of the remaining expected redeemable pool from all issuance cohorts, so lifetime redemption and timing assumptions do not double count. No cash inflow is assumed when points are redeemed. Contribution excludes program administration, incremental spend, acquisition costs, taxes, and other costs.
